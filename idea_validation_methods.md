# Idea Validation — What 170 Founders Actually Did

**Source:** 167 Starter Story transcripts + 3 Superwall (SuperwallHQ) long-form transcripts, analyzed end-to-end.
**Generated:** 2026-04-17.

This is the practical answer to the question "how do I stop guessing whether my idea will work?" — synthesized from how real founders actually validated ideas that ended up making $10K–$48M.

---

## The one rule that cuts through everything

> **Validation = "Did a stranger hand over their money, attention, or 30 minutes on a call — *before* I finished the product?"**

Every reliable validation method in the 170 videos maps to that sentence. Everything else — mom-test surveys, "would you use this?" polls, friends saying "cool idea," free users — is **theatre**. Multiple founders say this almost verbatim:

- **Gil (Subscribr):** "The only way to really get validation is to collect money from people."
- **Teemu (Setter AI):** "The only way to validate is when buyers swipe their credit card."
- **Devin (Supergrow):** "Free users don't actually validate your product."
- **Angus (Bank Statement Converter):** "Don't show it to your friends and family — they will just lie to you."
- **Florin Pop:** "Add monetization early. Add a paid tier. **This is the real validation strategy**. Do people pay? If they do, then you have something valuable."

Keep that rule in your head. Everything below is tactics for surfacing that proof cheaply.

## The 3 validation moves that actually worked — ranked by evidence

### TIER 1A — Dollar-validation (pre-sell before you build)

**What:** Put a real Stripe/Gumroad buy button in front of target users with only a landing page, demo video, or fake screens behind it. Count how many pay.

**Why it beats everything else:** You filter for buyer intent, not curiosity. A $5 pre-order is worth 1,000 "I'd love this" replies.

**Concrete examples from the data:**

| Founder | Product | What they did | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Caleb Dean | Runify | ChatGPT-built landing page + Stripe $5 early-adopter link + TikTok rank-reveal videos | 2,000 waitlist, 90+ paid, 3,000 pre-orders → later $100K exit in 26 days |
| Teemu / Joseph | Setter AI | Fake landing page with fake 11Labs demo + Calendly, "100% refundable deposit" | $500 paid deposit *before* a line of code |
| Gil | Subscribr | 50-license pre-sale, tiered ($X, then +$Y every 10) with money-back guarantee | $20,000 in 3 days *before* the product existed |
| Aaron Spivak | Hush Blankets | Pre-order page + Google Ads on "weighted blankets" → Kickstarter | $25K goal → $1M+ in 30 days |
| Umberto | Floa / Playosb | Lifetime deal launch with 3 price tiers + email list warm-up | $120,000 in 24 hours |
| Mark Lou | ShipFast | Cold-emailed 1 person before building; she said "send me the invoice" | First dollar before any product |
| Pat Walls | Starter Story premium | Loom demo video + Stripe link | $20K in pre-orders in 1 day |
| Pauline | AI Crea (real estate) | Showed MVP to a few agents personally; those who said "I want it now" paid | First-dollar validation |
| Colin | Sheets & Giggles | Email capture + survey + wireframes, then crowdfunded | Hit crowdfunding goal because pre-launch math was already proven |

**Gate rule (adapt it):** If you can't get 5 strangers to hand over real money against a landing page + demo video, don't build the product.

### TIER 1B — Clone a winner (piggyback on proven demand)

**What:** Find a product already making $X00K+/mo. Don't invent the category — pick the same category, with a twist (better UX, tighter niche, different platform, different pricing).

**Why it works:** The hardest risk — "does anyone want this at all?" — is already resolved by the competitor's revenue.

**The explicit threshold rules founders use:**

- **Caleb Dean (Runify) — 3-part filter:**
  1. At least 1–3 competitors doing **$100K+/mo**
  2. Sustained revenue **≥12 months** (not a blip)
  3. **Not dominated by a single VC-funded player** (their paid-ad economics don't translate to bootstrappers)
- **Max (28-app portfolio):** Keyword popularity ≥20%, difficulty 60–70%, **competitor revenue ≥€100–200/month minimum**.
- **Mal Baron (Prayer Lock):** "Look for niches with apps doing **over $50K/mo**, use Sensor Tower."
- **Lots (app flipper):** Scan App Store top 20; if **all of the top 3–5 are doing $100K–500K MRR**, there's room.
- **Samuel (Artemis, StoryShort) — 4-filter framework:** (1) I'd use it myself, (2) it already works for someone, (3) they're **not burning on marketing** (= real organic demand), (4) simple to maintain. Hunts for "MRR screenshots" on Twitter as the cleanest signal.
- **Will Cannon (UpLead, Signaturely):** Clones proven markets (cloned ZoomInfo, cloned DocuSign), differentiates by **mining 1-and-2-star reviews** of the incumbents.
- **Samuel (again):** Uses **Ahrefs** to check a competitor's traffic mix. Ads + SEO = strong demand. Ads only = fragile.
- **Oliver Brocato (Tabs Chocolate):** Saw a competitor's TikTok at 8M views, 2M likes, **with no website**. Cloned the niche, did $280K in 3 weeks.

**Tools founders named explicitly:**
- Sensor Tower, AppMagic, App Annie — competitor revenue / downloads (iOS/Android)
- Ahrefs, Keywords Everywhere, VidIQ — SEO / YouTube keyword volume + difficulty
- Google Trends — directional trend stability (Aaron Spivak found 300K/mo weighted-blanket searches here)
- MicroAcquire — Adrian filters by "SaaS, asking price ≥$300K" to find proven SaaS ideas to clone
- App Store search suggestions + top-20 ranks
- GummySearch, redditlist.com — growing subreddits
- G2 / Capterra / AppSumo / App Store reviews — mine 1★ reviews for the gap

**Kill criterion:** If nobody is making money in this niche, that's not a "greenfield opportunity" — it's almost always **evidence the market doesn't want it**. Thomas (Unlaunched): *"Not having competitors means there's no market."*

### TIER 1C — Concierge / Wizard-of-Oz (validate by delivering manually)

**What:** Do the job by hand for your first 5–10 customers before you automate. The product is a human in a T-shirt; the "app" is a Typeform + you.

**Why it works:** You learn the real workflow, find what they'll pay for, and validate feature priority — none of which you can do from a landing page.

**Concrete examples:**
- **John Rush (20+ apps):** Explicit blueprint step 6: *"I don't even build a product. I actually deliver the solution manually. It's much easier to iterate when there is no product."*
- **Nico (MakeLogo, 17 apps):** Backend was a Typeform for weeks. No code, just a founder.
- **Jordan (Parakeet Chat / prison AI):** MVP and validation were the same thing — he hand-carried demos to prison contacts and "within a month had 200 paying users."
- **Andre (Listkit):** Ran the service manually for $5–10K/mo first, *then* built the SaaS with feature priorities informed by that run.
- **Joseph (Super Demo):** Offered to manually make demos for founders on Reddit/Indie Hackers to learn the workflow.
- **Colin Macintosh (SheetsResume):** Gave free resume reviews on Reddit for years, then thousands of free resume builds, *then* enabled payment.
- **David Bressler (Formula Bot):** Got on Zoom calls and did customers' Excel work for free in exchange for feedback.
- **Dicky Bush (Ship 30 for 30):** First ghost-writing client paid *after* the work — "pay me what you think it's worth." Got $5K for a job he'd have quoted at $500. Willingness-to-pay validated 10×.

**Gate rule:** If you can't find 5 people who'll pay you to do the thing manually, an app version of the thing isn't going to get customers either.

## TIER 2 — Cheap signal-gathering (no hard validation on its own, but directionally useful)

These are early tripwires that tell you *whether to run the Tier-1 moves*. None of them prove anything without a paid customer at the end.

### 2A. The Reddit / niche community post

The single most-repeated cheap validation move. **15+ founders** used it.

- **Lucas (Stage Timer):** "A **single Reddit post** with 58 upvotes changed everything" — launched a niche-subreddit post with "give me feedback, what do you think?"
- **Dennis (Yaphone):** Reddit launch tied to a viral Peter Levels tweet → "I got my first sales in minutes, which never happened before."
- **Anish (SaveWise) — 5-step Reddit playbook:**
  1. Lurk in the sub silently
  2. Start in comments, not posts
  3. Set **F5Bot keyword alerts** for your target-user conversations
  4. Post only after you've earned trust
  5. Launch links last, not first
- **Leandro (Synto Sheets):** Searched r/notion for "sheets, google sheets, excel, csv" → found 100+ people asking for exactly his product.
- **Steph France — "Gold Mining":** Google advanced-search Reddit for `"wish there was an app for" site:reddit.com`, paste threads into Claude, extract verbatim pain-point quotes, *use those pain-point quotes as the landing-page copy*.
- **David Bressler (Formula Bot):** Posted to r/Excel → top post of the day. Posted to r/InternetIsBeautiful → 10K upvotes → thousands of users.

### 2B. The one-tweet demand probe

Cheapest form of fake-door on the planet.

- **Alex Lieberman (Storyarb):** *"I put out a tweet saying 'if you're an executive who believes in the power of building audience, DM me.' I got 25 DMs. That's my favorite way to build a business: product doesn't exist yet, you get a sense of demand, then you build the product."*
- **Mark (Papermark):** Tweeted "I'm going to build an open-source alternative to Docsend" → 40K views in hours.
- **Push School (Alejandro & Mario):** Made a **fake product demo video** using a random AI push-up detection clip from YouTube — 80K views, 500 comments "please build this." Built in 2 weeks.
- **CJ (CodeGuide):** Ideal-workflow tweet → ~400 RTs → **1,800 waitlist signups in 2 weeks** from a Bolt-built landing page. Zero product existed.
- **Polus (Creator Hunter):** *"If you put a buy button on it, share it on Twitter in the scrappiest way, you'll be surprised by the amount of people that actually want to get it."*

### 2C. The fake-door paid-ads test

- **Angus (Bank Statement Converter):** Built MVP in 1 week → **Google Ads to the landing page** → "people immediately started uploading bank statements, which kind of validated the problem."
- **Aaron Spivak (Hush Blankets):** Bare pre-order site + Google Ads on the most expensive keyword. Validation came via ad response rate.
- **Samuel (StoryShort):** *"I always start by running ads. It's the fastest way to validate and test the market."*
- **Mickey (Late):** Google Ads at $120 target CPA — "paid search gives us immediate results and feedback." Treats ad spend as the price of buying honest data.

### 2D. TikTok/IG content-first demand test

Faster than ads, free, and double-duty as future paid-ad creative.

- **Push School:** Fake demo video → 80K views → build.
- **Louis (Glow Up makeup AI):** Mined TikTok comments for pain; went content-first before building.
- **Caleb Dean (Runify):** **Posted ~50 short videos in 2 weeks** on a cloned competitor's format *before* the app existed → 2,000 waitlist.
- **Ethan (Cut Coach):** Organic TikToks got 200–500 views but **10–15 installs each** → "very high-intent viewers" proved the niche before scaling.
- **Steven (Puff Count):** Search TikTok for the niche, filter "most liked all time," scroll 7 days, log hook/storyline/CTA for each viral video. Only post once pattern is clear. *"Before I posted a single video on the PuffCount TikTok, I scrolled for 7 days straight."*
- **Connor (Payout):** *"Go look on TikTok and see if anyone's talking about the problem. Look at the comments and see if people are asking how do I solve this problem?"*
- **Blake Anderson (Rizz GPT / Umax):** Creates a fake target-persona TikTok account ("Cal") and scrolls purely the target-market FYP for a week to reverse-engineer what converts.

### 2E. Customer interviews (but only if done right)

The Mom Test / Umberto rule: "never tell them why you're asking, you want an unbiased reaction."

- **Umberto (Floa):** *"Talk to 5 to 10 people in your target market. Never tell them why you're asking. You want an unbiased honest reaction."*
- **Joseph (Super Demo):** Talked to 100+ founders to find "the unifying concern was — yes, this was a really painful problem."
- **Aaron Spivak (Hush Blankets):** Called **every churned customer** via Calendly link. Found the sweating problem. Pivoted to cooling blanket.
- **David Park (Jenny AI):** *"Don't ask why they like your product, ask why they dislike it, and what they love about competitors."*
- **Guam Moubes (Lemlist):** Closed the **first 100 customers via live demo**. Direct conversation = validation + feedback.
- **Dicky Bush:** 50 one-on-one interviews before launching the $99 productized course.

**Interview kill rule (Angus):** If they're friends or family, the data is poisoned. Talk to strangers in your ICP, or don't do it.

### 2F. Scratch your own itch (founder-as-user)

Most common underlying theme: **60%+ of successful founders in this dataset were their own first customer.** It's not a validation method on its own, but it's an evidence-multiplier for every other step.

The rule (Sebastian, Habit Kit): *"I just build apps that solve my own problems. I will always be my first customer."*

The strongest pattern: **founder-as-user + observed echoes of the same pain elsewhere** (forums, DMs, Reddit).
- Ben (FollowBuddy): kept getting DMs asking about Instagram unfollowers
- Ivan (Lancer): internal agency tool for Upwork bidding that other agencies wanted too
- Thomas (Packager): his own IT-admin pain + "forums full of IT admins with the exact same problem"
- Angus: his own bank-PDF pain was hard enough to solve that *"it must be a real problem"*

## TIER 3 — What does NOT count as validation (kill these instincts)

| Anti-pattern | Why it fails | Who called it out |
|---|---|---|
| "Would you pay for this?" surveys | People lie. Intent ≠ action. | Gil, Teemu |
| Friends & family feedback | Social pressure contaminates data | Angus |
| Free signups / waitlist clicks without a price tag | Curiosity ≠ buyer intent | Devin, Leandro |
| YouTube/TikTok view counts | "40M views on a novelty video converts worse than 10M views on a solution video" | Brett & Zach (Coconote) |
| Product Hunt #1 | Fun spike, doesn't pay rent | Anish (SaveWise failed on PH/HN/IH first) |
| Building for months before testing distribution | Biggest regret across the dataset | Caleb Dean, Cole (Roblox dev who burned 3 years / $300K) |
| A single competitor (monopoly) | Could be a one-off VC bet, not a real market | Caleb Dean |
| A bunch of VC-funded competitors only | Their unit economics won't map to you | Caleb Dean |

## 5 ready-to-use validation playbooks

Pick one. Execute it end-to-end in a week.

### Playbook A — "Caleb Dean's 7-day app test" (best for mobile app ideas)
1. **Day 1:** Find a competitor on **Sensor Tower** doing $100K+/mo for ≥12 months, not VC-funded.
2. **Day 1–2:** Identify their winning short-form format (IG Reels, TikTok). Copy the structure exactly.
3. **Day 2:** Use ChatGPT to write a one-page landing site. Add a Stripe link for a **$5 "early-adopter" slot** + waitlist form. Publish on a $12 domain.
4. **Day 2–7:** Post **50 videos** across 1–2 new accounts in their format, always ending with a CTA to your landing page.
5. **Day 7 gate:** If <50 paid $5 slots, **kill it**. If >50, move the paid users into a WhatsApp/Discord, start building, ship MVP in 2 weeks.

### Playbook B — "Gil's pre-sale" (best for software where you have any audience)
1. Write a 1-page sales page describing the outcome (not the features).
2. Set up a **tiered pre-sale**: first 10 slots at $X, next 10 at $X+Y, etc. **Money-back guarantee** + stated build deadline.
3. Announce to your audience (email / X / LinkedIn).
4. **Kill rule:** If the first tier (10 cheapest slots) doesn't sell in 72 hours, the idea is too niche for you right now.

### Playbook C — "John Rush's 7-step cold blueprint" (best if you have no audience)
1. Solve a pain you personally have.
2. Search for existing solutions. If none exist and nobody's complaining, **walk away** — pain probably isn't real.
3. Post about the pain publicly (tweet, Reddit, LinkedIn). Watch for resonance.
4. Launch a waitlist page. Target: **100 signups**.
5. Email the list a **90%-off pre-sale**.
6. If 5 people buy, build MVP.
7. For those 5, **deliver the solution manually** (concierge) until they love it. *Then* code.

### Playbook D — "Steph France's Gold Mining" (best if you don't have an idea yet)
1. Open **Keywords Everywhere**. Find 5–10 problem keywords in your area of interest with 10K+/mo search volume.
2. Check each on **Google Trends** — want stable or growing, not flash.
3. Google advanced-search **Reddit** for the phrases (`site:reddit.com "I wish there was"`). Collect 20+ threads.
4. Paste the threads into Claude. Prompt: "extract every pain point, with verbatim customer quotes."
5. Build a **Lovable landing page** using the verbatim quotes as headlines.
6. Add a waitlist popup quiz. Seed with 1–2 niche Reddit/FB posts linking to it.
7. **Gate:** 100 waitlist signups → build. 15% conversion on landing page = passing.

### Playbook E — "Oliver Brocato's viral-with-no-website" (best for DTC / physical / consumer)
1. Scroll TikTok in your category. Find any video with **1M+ views and a broken/missing website link**.
2. That's demand someone failed to capture.
3. Shopify + a couple of quality product photos. Pre-order mode.
4. Post **in the exact content format that blew up for them**.
5. **Gate:** If Week 1 doesn't sell out initial 100 units, the mismatch is on your execution, not demand.

## The paywall/post-launch validation layer (from Superwall videos)

Once you're past the "will anyone buy?" question, the validation doesn't stop — it just moves to funnel metrics. Brett (Coconote) and Kobe (Sunflower) are explicit:

**Healthy numbers founders target:**
- **Install → paywall view** > "double this metric and you double revenue" (Jake Mor, Superwall CEO's rule).
- **Trial start rate** as a function of onboarding length: Brett doubled onboarding from 5 → 13 screens, got +16% trial conversion. Longer, higher-commitment onboarding seems to help.
- **Day-30 retention** ≥ 60% for consumer apps (Pray Screen cited by Lots as a "60% D30, which is kind of unheard of").
- **Trial → paid** — if you see 45% trial start but only 13% convert, **the product is broken, not the distribution** (Eyal, Prop GPT).
- **Cancel-flow save rate** — offering a 7-day trial extension saved Brett 27% of cancellations.
- **CAC:LTV** ≥ 3:1 before scaling paid ads (Steven, Puff Count).
- **Conversion rate on ASO** — Caleb's Yuri/Brave got 30%+ from super-niche keywords. "The app store already did the validation."

**Price-sensitivity rule (Kobe, Sunflower):** *"We made a lot of gains by actually lowering our price than increasing it."* Test downward, not just upward.

**Paywall ladder that worked for Sunflower ($0→$1M/yr in 10 months):**
1. Monthly-only → add annual → default to annual → trial on/off toggle → trial reminders.
2. That single ladder delivered: +48% revenue, +70% annual revenue, +46% trial conversion, +30% trial starts.

## The decision flowchart — "If I got an idea today, here's what I'd do"

```
Got an idea
    |
    v
Do I personally feel this pain right now?
    | No  --> Kill it or ride-along on someone else's pain (customer interviews)
    | Yes
    v
Does >=1 competitor make >= $100K/mo for >=12 months and isn't purely VC-funded?
    | No  --> 95% chance the market is too small. Kill or re-niche.
    | Yes
    v
Can I find 20+ forum/Reddit/TikTok-comment posts of people expressing this exact pain?
    | No  --> Weak signal. Interview 10 strangers first.
    | Yes
    v
Ship a $12 domain + one-page Bolt/Lovable site + Stripe buy button + 60-second demo video
(whether the "product" exists or not — concierge MVP is fine).
    |
    v
Post 10 short-form videos / Reddit comments / cold DMs per day for 7 days.
    |
    v
How many strangers paid real money in 7 days?
    | 0       --> Idea is dead. Archive it. Next.
    | 1-4     --> Interview all of them for 30 min. Re-test the hook/offer. 1 more week.
    | 5-20    --> You have signal. Build MVP. Keep pre-selling while you build.
    | 20+     --> Stop taking pre-orders, start shipping, start hiring.
```

## Quick reference — the evidence I'd bet money on

If I had to pick the **single move that appeared in the most successful stories** in this dataset, it's this one:

**Find a proven competitor doing real revenue, copy their winning short-form video format, send traffic to a landing page with a real buy button, and count dollars — all before writing production code.**

That move — or some variant of it — showed up in at least **30+ of the 170 transcripts**, across SaaS, mobile apps, DTC, and info products. It compresses competitor validation, demand validation, distribution validation, and dollar validation into one 7-day sprint.

That's what I'd do.
